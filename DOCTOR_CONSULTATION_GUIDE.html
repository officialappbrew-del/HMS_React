History of Present Illness Required

Document the patient's chief complaint and symptom details

What to document: The patient's story in their own words, including the timeline and nature of their symptoms.

Chief Complaint Duration Onset Location Severity Character Radiation Associated Symptoms Aggravating Factors Relieving Factors Previous Treatment Progression

Example: "Patient reports severe headache for 3 days, located in the frontal region, rated 8/10 in severity. Aggravated by light and noise. Relieved by rest. No previous treatment."

Clinical Best Practice: Complete the HPI first to establish clinical context. Use the structured fields for key data points and the free-text area for narrative detail.

Medications & Allergies Required

Reconcile current medications and document allergies

Why this matters: Medication reconciliation prevents errors and identifies potential drug-allergy interactions.

To add a medication, provide:

  • Name — Drug name (e.g., "Amlodipine 5mg")
  • Dosage — Strength and unit (e.g., "500mg")
  • Frequency — When to take (e.g., "Twice daily", "Once daily")
  • Duration — How long to take (e.g., "7 days", "Ongoing")
  • Route — How to take (Oral, IV, IM, Topical)
  • Reason — Clinical indication

To document an allergy, provide:

  • Type: Drug, Food, Environmental, Other
  • Substance: Name of allergen (e.g., "Penicillin")
  • Severity: Severe, Moderate, Mild
  • Reaction: Rash, Anaphylaxis, etc.
  • Notes: Additional clinical context
Add Medication Add Allergy Remove Collapse

Safety Alert: The system automatically checks for drug-allergy interactions and displays clinical alerts in the safety panel at the top right.

Clinical Note: Always verify medication history with the patient and family members. Use the pharmacy records when available.

Diagnosis (ICD-10) Required

Search and assign standardized diagnosis codes

How to use:

  1. Type a diagnosis code or description into the search bar
  2. Review the results from the ICD-10 database
  3. Click "Add" next to a code to assign it to the encounter
  4. Selected codes appear in the right panel
  5. Click "Remove" to unassign a code

Why ICD-10 matters: These codes are essential for billing, statistical reporting, and clinical decision support.

Treatment Plan Required

Document the comprehensive care plan

What to include in the treatment plan:

Management Plan Medications Lifestyle Advice Dietary Advice Patient Education Monitoring Plan Safety Net Advice

Example treatment plan:

  • Management: Start Amoxicillin 500mg TDS for 7 days
  • Lifestyle: Rest and increased fluid intake
  • Monitoring: Review in 3 days if symptoms persist
  • Safety net: Return to ED if fever >39°C or worsening symptoms

Best Practice: A well-documented treatment plan improves patient outcomes and reduces readmission rates.

Disposition & Follow-up Required

Plan patient discharge, admission, or referral

Select the disposition type:

  • Outpatient — Discharged for home care with follow-up
  • Inpatient — Admitted to hospital for further care
  • Observation — Short-term monitoring
  • Referral — Referred to another provider or specialist

For admission:

  • Select Yes or No
  • Provide the reason for admission

Schedule follow-up:

  • Date — Recommended follow-up date
  • Time — Preferred time
  • Reason — Clinical indication for follow-up

Electronic Signature Required

Finalize the consultation with your digital signature

Quick Actions:

ActionWhat it does
Print Summary Opens a printable patient summary for your records
Sign & Close Finalizes the consultation with your digital signature
Save Consult Saves the entire consultation to the system

Electronic Signature:

  • Displays your name and license number
  • Shows signed status and timestamp
  • Once signed, the consultation is locked and moved to billing

Important: Sign-off is a legal action. Ensure all clinical documentation is complete before signing.

Clinical Alerts & Audit Safety

Safety warnings and action logging

Clinical Alerts (auto-generated):

  • High Risk — Medication-allergy interactions
  • Moderate Risk — Potential drug interactions
  • Info — Missing prescriptions during active encounters

Example Alert: "Prescription Amoxicillin may trigger patient allergy to Penicillin. Consider alternative antibiotic."

Audit Trail:

  • Records all actions with timestamps
  • Includes: medication changes, allergy updates, saves, sign-offs
  • Essential for medico-legal documentation

Compliance: The audit trail provides a complete record of all clinical decisions and changes for regulatory compliance.
